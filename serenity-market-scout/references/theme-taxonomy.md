## AI optical interconnect and CPO

Core question: which companies control the optical path from AI accelerators to rack-scale and cluster-scale communication?

Subcomponents to scan: CW lasers, external light sources, FAU, optical engines, pluggable modules, CPO packaging, optical inspection, photonic substrates, InP wafers, cage thermal, connectors, transceivers, and test equipment.

Seed names for research, not recommendations: Sivers/SIVE/SIVEF, AAOI, LITE, COHR, POET, MRVL, JBL, MTSI, FOCI 3363, Shunsin 6451, MSSCorp 6830, AXTI, IQE, XFAB, SOI/Soitec, Win Semiconductor 3105.

Key checks: customer qualification, 1.6T/3.2T ramp, laser capacity, foundry partner capacity, gross margin, supply agreements, dual sourcing, revenue timing, dilution.

## HBM4 and advanced packaging

Core question: which tools and materials become necessary as HBM moves to HBM4/HBM4E and advanced bonding?

Subcomponents to scan: compression molding, hybrid bonding, IR metrology, overlay tools, wafer inspection, bonding/debonding, high-end substrates, underfill, thermal interface materials, and test equipment.

Seed names: TOWA 6315, Auros 322310, KLAC, AMAT, ONTO, CAMT, AEHR, Tazmo 6266, BESI, TER, Advantest, Unimicron, Ibiden, Hanmi Semiconductor, ISC.

Key checks: Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron adoption, HBM4 timing, tool qualification, capex cycle, customer concentration, and whether the supplier's niche is material.

## Glass core substrates and ABF

Core question: which tool, material, and inspection suppliers benefit if AI GPUs, ASICs, and CPO create another substrate shortage?

Subcomponents to scan: LIDE/TGV equipment, glass materials, photoresists, etch systems, dicing, bonding, metrology, inspection, ABF substrate capacity.

Seed names: LPKF/LPK, GLW, AGC, Nippon Electric Glass, LRCX, DISCO, ONTO, KLAC, YC Chem, Samyang NC Chem, Unimicron, Ibiden, Shinko.

Key checks: mass production year, customer qualification, tool count per customer, ASP per tool, gross margin, and whether orders are committed or speculative.

## Critical materials and supply-chain sovereignty

Core question: which materials become strategic because AI, robotics, defense, or photonics cannot scale without them?

Subcomponents to scan: InP, SiC, GaN, rare earths, scandium, yttrium, neodymium, indium, specialty glass, photoresists, advanced ceramics, thermal materials.

Seed names: AXTI, IQE, MP, Lynas, ALRIB/Riber, CPSH, CoorsTek if accessible, specialty chemical suppliers by local market.

Key checks: mine-to-wafer control, refining capacity, export controls, government grants, customer qualification, and whether the company can monetize scarcity without destroying demand.

## Space, satellites, and defense hardware

Core question: which small suppliers benefit when LEO satellite constellations, space-based connectivity, directed energy, and defense electronics move from prototype to production?

Subcomponents to scan: optical terminals, RF front ends, radiation-hardened electronics, power systems, specialty ceramics, precision thermal hardware, laser components, ground-station equipment, and advanced materials.

Seed names: MDA Space, Rocket Lab, Redwire, Mynaric if still viable, MKS Instruments, Leonardo DRS, Mercury Systems, CPI, CPSH, specialty photonics and RF suppliers by local market.

Key checks: funded programs, backlog quality, launch cadence, customer concentration, defense procurement timing, export-control limits, financing risk, and whether the supplier has real production content.

Seed names: Murata, TDK, Taiyo Yuden, Yageo, Kyocera, Vishay, Bel Fuse, Powell, Hammond Power, Eaton, Hubbell, nVent, Schneider, Siemens.

## Healthcare, life-science tools, and regulated capacity

Core question: which companies control the narrow point between clinical demand, manufacturing capacity, reimbursement, and regulatory approval?

Subcomponents to scan: sterile fill-finish, CDMO capacity, diagnostics platforms, specialty reagents, clinical trial tools, reimbursement infrastructure, radiopharma isotopes, cold-chain logistics, and regulated data networks.

Seed names are not fixed. Build the list from current approvals, shortages, reimbursement changes, FDA or EMA documents, company filings, and customer capex.

Key checks: clinical and regulatory risk, reimbursement, capacity utilization, customer concentration, manufacturing quality, payer behavior, and whether the public company exposure is material.

## Energy, water, and infrastructure constraints

Core question: which physical constraints become unavoidable as electrification, data centers, reshoring, defense production, and climate adaptation compete for capacity?

Subcomponents to scan: transformers, switchgear, grid interconnection, turbines, compressors, water treatment, desalination, pumps, valves, meters, permitting services, specialty construction, and environmental compliance.

Seed names are not fixed. Build the list from utility plans, government procurement, backlog disclosures, public tenders, and industrial capex plans.

Key checks: backlog quality, margin durability, project timing, permitting, working-capital needs, cyclicality, and exposure to public spending.

## Financial infrastructure, compliance, and market structure

Core question: which companies benefit when regulation, settlement, payments, collateral, market data, custody, or compliance costs change the industry structure?

Subcomponents to scan: exchanges, clearing, custody, compliance software, stablecoin issuers, payment networks, tax and reporting infrastructure, insurance brokers, data vendors, and capital-markets utilities.

Seed names are not fixed. Build the list from legislation, regulator statements, exchange filings, payment-volume data, and company disclosures.

Key checks: regulation, take rate, float economics, interest-rate sensitivity, license durability, network effects, and incumbent competition.

## Consumer, distribution, and local-market bottlenecks

Core question: which overlooked distributors, service networks, or local monopolies capture a demand shift better than the headline brand?

Subcomponents to scan: specialty distributors, repair networks, installers, used-equipment markets, local logistics, franchise systems, procurement platforms, and category-specific marketplaces.

Seed names are not fixed. Build the list from sell-through data, channel checks, company filings, local-market disclosures, and supplier-customer maps.

Key checks: pricing power, working capital, channel inventory, customer retention, local competition, cyclicality, and whether growth is organic or acquisition-driven.

## How to use this taxonomy

Start with three to five themes, then reduce to the best two after source verification. A theme deserves priority only when it has live evidence, a near-term catalyst, and identifiable public equities. Do not include every seed name in the final output; use the taxonomy as a scanning map. When the user asks for unconstrained discovery, do not begin from this taxonomy alone; use it after the first open-ended scan to test whether obvious candidate areas were missed.
