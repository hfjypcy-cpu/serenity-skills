## Purpose

Use this when the user wants the skill to discover the next promising industry, concept, or stock without preselecting a sector. The built-in theme taxonomy is only a seed map. Do not limit the search to AI, semiconductors, CPO, HBM, glass substrates, power, cooling, robotics, or stablecoins.

## Discovery rule

Start with the market structure, not with a favorite industry. A sector becomes interesting only when current evidence shows a large demand or policy force, a changing architecture, a narrow capture point, identifiable public equities, and a catalyst window that can matter to public markets.

Good open-ended themes can come from any sector, including technology, industrials, energy, materials, healthcare tools, biotech platforms, financial infrastructure, consumer distribution, defense, transportation, agriculture, water, waste, construction, insurance, exchanges, data providers, and local-market supply chains.

## Cross-sector scan

Build the first pass from current sources:

- Earnings-call language: demand inflections, capacity constraints, backlog, pricing power, margin expansion, and customer qualification.
- Capex and procurement: government awards, grid plans, defense contracts, data-center buildouts, manufacturing reshoring, hospital capex, logistics automation, and infrastructure tenders.
- Regulation and policy: laws, standards, tariffs, subsidies, reimbursement changes, listing rules, capital requirements, and compliance mandates.
- Technology and architecture: new manufacturing flows, new standards, new system designs, new drugs or diagnostics, new payment rails, and new distribution channels.
- Shortage and lead-time clues: components, labor, permitting, power, water, land, specialized tools, safety certifications, and regulated capacity.
- Market neglect: small caps, non-US listings, old-economy labels, hidden segments, spinoffs, busted IPOs, and companies covered under the wrong peer group.

The first pass should produce several candidate themes. Then reduce the list to the few with the strongest evidence and timing.

## First-pass filters

Prioritize a theme when these conditions line up:

- There is current evidence, not only a narrative.
- The constraint is narrow enough for a company to capture value.
- The public company exposure is material or could become material.
- The catalyst is visible within roughly 6-24 months.
- The stock is not already fully priced for the thesis.
- The downside risks can be named and monitored.

Reject or defer a theme when it has no public equity capture, the proof depends on rumors, the timing is too far away, or the best stocks have already rerated without new evidence.

## From sector to stock

Once a theme passes, map it in layers:

1. End demand or policy force.
2. System or market-structure change.
3. Bottleneck or functional monopoly.
4. Company roles: direct winner, supplier, tool vendor, materials vendor, distributor, compliance infrastructure, data layer, or capacity owner.
5. Evidence quality.
6. Catalyst.
7. Valuation and risk.

For sectors outside physical supply chains, translate bottleneck logic into the relevant constraint. In healthcare this may be reimbursement, clinical data, manufacturing capacity, distribution, or regulatory exclusivity. In finance it may be license, compliance cost, float economics, liquidity, or network access. In energy and infrastructure it may be permitting, interconnection, grid equipment, land, water, or specialized labor.

## Rejection log

For open-ended work, include a short rejection log. It should name themes that were considered but excluded because the evidence was weak, timing was too early, public equities were poor captures, or valuation had already adjusted.
