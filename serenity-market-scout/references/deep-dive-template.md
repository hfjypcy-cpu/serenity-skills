## When to use

Use this when the user asks for one company, one ticker, or one thesis to be researched in depth. The output should read like a research memo built from current sources and explicit inference, not a generic company profile.

## Required sections

Cover these sections in order:

1. One-line thesis: the exact bottleneck or mismatch.
2. Terminal demand: the downstream demand shock that can make the niche matter.
3. Architecture shift: why the old supply chain or old comps miss the new winner.
4. Bottleneck role: the product, process, material, capacity, certification, or customer access that is hard to bypass.
5. Customer map: confirmed and inferred customers with confidence tiers.
6. Capacity and moat: manufacturing partner, foundry, IP, yield, qualification, backlog, grants, or process know-how.
7. Catalyst calendar: next 6-24 months of events that can move perception.
8. Valuation mismatch: why the current market framing may be stale, with current market data verified when practical.
9. Thesis-breakers: dilution, dual sourcing, qualification failure, customer loss, overvaluation, timing, or liquidity.
10. Verification list: the few checks needed before treating the idea as actionable.

## Customer map table

Recommended columns:

- Customer or end customer
- Link to company
- Confidence tier
- Supporting evidence
- Missing proof
- Potential materiality

Do not blend confirmed and inferred customers in prose. Put the distinction in the table.

## Catalyst calendar

Recommended columns:

- Date or window
- Catalyst
- Source status
- Why it matters
- What would disappoint

Useful catalyst types include product qualification, new customer confirmation, foundry ramp, capacity expansion, government funding, standards adoption, large customer capex, design win, revenue guide, and M&A interest.

## Risk and invalidation

Every deep dive should name a principal thesis-breaker. For small caps, always check:

- Liquidity.
- Dilution, ATM, converts, warrants, or going-concern language.
- Customer concentration.
- Whether the product is still sampling instead of producing.
- Whether gross margin targets depend on scale that has not arrived.
- Whether a large customer can dual source or vertically integrate.

## Output pattern

Use this structure for Chinese output:

```
研究边界

一句话结论

核心逻辑

客户与证据链表

催化剂时间表

估值错配假设

最大风险和证伪条件

下一步核验清单
```

Keep the reasoning in paragraphs and use tables for evidence-heavy comparisons.
